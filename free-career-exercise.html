10-minute sample

Translate one research achievement.

Your goal: show an engineering hiring manager what you personally did, why it mattered and how you know it worked—without inventing metrics or overstating ownership.

STEP 01 / RAW EXPERIENCE

Start with the activity

Example: “Conducted thin-film experiments and used SEM, XRD and electrical testing in doctoral research.”

This identifies methods, but not the problem, decisions or outcome.

STEP 02 / ENGINEERING EVIDENCE

Ask four questions

What problem? Which methods did you choose? What was your ownership? What observed result can you defend (even if qualitative)?

STEP 03 / REWRITE

Make it concrete

Illustrative structure: “Investigated [specific device/process issue] by designing [experiment], applying [relevant measurement methods], and comparing [verified result] to guide [next engineering decision].”

Replace brackets only with your genuine work.

One technical interview prompt

Scenario: An electrical device metric worsens after a process split. What evidence would you collect before attributing the change to the process?

A useful answer explains the baseline, sample size, measurement repeatability, control wafer or lot, process differences, potential confounders, physical mechanisms, and how to falsify competing explanations. The goal is engineering reasoning, not memorizing an employer's confidential interview bank.

Your mini assignment: Write one genuine, outcome-focused bullet from your own thesis, internship or postdoc. Do not include confidential datasets or unpublished employer information.
